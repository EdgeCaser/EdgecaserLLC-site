The first roadmap question after an AI prototype is not "what else can it do?"

It is: what evidence would change the next build decision?

That question disappears quickly.

A demo can draft, classify, route, or summarize. Then the feature list blooms. Sales wants one extension. Support wants another. Engineering spots the retrieval debt. The founder stares at a roadmap that is suddenly larger than the company.

A 2026 arXiv analysis of more than 160,000 Product Hunt launches found a sharp rise in new launches after ChatGPT 3.5, with solo founders driving much of the jump. That tracks with what I see: AI lowers the cost of trying something.

It does not create product judgment.

Before the next roadmap session, I would ask:

What user behavior would make us build this next?
What failure pattern would make us stop?
Where does human review change the product promise?
Which quality signal is strong enough to move engineering time?

If the team cannot answer those, the roadmap is probably storing possibilities instead of evidence.
